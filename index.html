Handral Poornakanta

PhD scholar, Mechanical Engineering, Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru

I study slender elastic structures — beams, rods and ribbons that bend, buckle and twist far beyond small deflections. My work is experimental: I design the rigs, build the instruments, and use the measurements to test mechanical models, then turn what we learn into devices. I work with Prof. Ramsharan Rangarajan, and before my PhD I spent five years at IISc as a research associate building flexible robots and vision-based measurement setups.

Research

Experimental techniques built on slender elastic structures: using their large deformations to measure, to move and to see.

Download video (MP4, 1080p)

Thermal expansion from post-buckled shapes

A heated beam held between fixed ends has nowhere to grow, so it buckles. Its post-buckled shape carries the thermal strain, so reading that geometry gives the coefficient of thermal expansion from a measurement of shape alone.

Acta Mechanica (2026); Talk at ICEM 2024

Download video (MP4, 1080p)

A cantilever beam as a cable robot

Pulling cables attached along an elastic cantilever bends it into a controllable shape. Using the mechanics of the elastica, we choose the loads that place the tip where we want it, turning a simple strip into a flexible robotic arm.

Journal of Applied Mechanics (2019); Extreme Mechanics Letters (2020); Indian patent 569440

Download video (MP4, 1080p)

Stereo measurement of large deformations

Two calibrated cameras locate ArUco markers on a strongly deformed strip in 3D. Partial reconstructions from several views share one frame, so they merge into the full shape, here a Möbius strip. Comparing these shapes with simulations shows which mechanical models for ribbons hold up and where they break down.

Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids (2020); Proceedings of the Royal Society A (2021)
